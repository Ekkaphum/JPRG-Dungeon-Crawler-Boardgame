- **kit1 now pays at most 2 ally hits per weak-point window** — the fix for BACKLOG §3b, which measured kit1 as a latent ~2x score swing sitting in the shipped v0.3 ruleset. - **v0.5.0 — the camp: gems, a shared item market, and three ways to spend** — a new opt-in ruleset layered on top of v0.4.0 (it keeps the ailments and the expanded roster). Between boss battles the party now stops at a camp with three sub-phases in a fixed order, each one's budget being whatever the previous left behind: **shop → upgrade → points**.
  - **Gems.** Every player gets the same payout: the boss's printed reward (**6 / 8 / 10**, announced on the boss sheet before the fight so the party can plan around what is coming) plus `floor(leftover slots / 3)`. **Gems do not carry between camps** — anything unspent at the end is destroyed. That single rule is what keeps the phase near three minutes: with savings allowed, every purchase becomes a cross-battle calculation, which is the difference between a 55-minute game and a two-hour one (`DESIGN_VARIABLES.md` §6.2).
  - **The market** is 4 cards face up plus a 1-card *future row* nobody may buy yet. Buying slides the future card into the empty slot and reveals a new one, so a purchase is also a decision about what you hand the next seat. Seats buy one at a time, **fewest points first, character speed breaking ties** — one rule doing the catch-up, the turn order and the tie-break at once, which is why no collision rule is needed.
  - **Items are a free action**: spent on your own visit, before you declare, any number, no ⏱ cost. Folded into the existing `DECLARE_ACTION` choice rather than given a new yield, so the turn order, the replay format and every bot keep the shape they already had — the cheapest possible reaction window. 15 of 17 types are consumable; the deck is 35 cards.
  - **Costs:** 8 gems per skill card flipped to Lv2, 4 gems per victory point. A `speed` stat was added to every character purely as the shopping tie-break — deliberately *not* a combat stat, since a global speed modifier would rescale every ⏱ in the game and void `BALANCE_NOTES` wholesale.
  - **Measured, then retuned once.** The first pass (12 gems per boss, `÷2` time bonus, 6 per upgrade) paid ~28 gems a game and pushed hard win rate 51.1% → **62.1%** — the camp was quietly doubling the game's progression. At 6/8/10, `÷3` and 8 it pays ~20 and lands at **52.0% against v0.4's 51.1%**, i.e. **power-neutral (+0.9pp)**. Medium: 68.8% against 67.4%. Ragorath's clear rate is *identical* across v0.4 and v0.5 (74.8%) — as it must be, since the first camp happens after him, which is the cleanest confirmation available that the phase is wired where it claims to be.
  - ⚠️ **Open finding, logged not fixed: Kit takes 60.2% of won games under v0.5, against 25.8% under v0.4.** The camp is *not* handing out more progression — measured flips fell from 1.50 to 1.18 per player per game. It hands out **different** progression: `campUpgradeDefault` ranks upgrades by damage-per-⏱, which puts Sharp Shooting first for Kit, where `placeExpDefault` picked Multi Shot. Lv2 Sharp Shooting lowers the weak-point roll target, and `kit1` ("open a weak point") is 1 point, per occurrence, **uncapped** — it fires 3.70 → 7.47 times a game and becomes 57% of Kit's score. Every other condition barely moves (eric2 3.29 → 2.99, liora1 2.19 → 2.25). This is at least partly **the bot's own heuristic being measured**, which this project has done by accident before, so nothing about Kit or the camp is being tuned on the strength of it. The real design exposure it points at is genuine and worth stating: any camp that reliably delivers a *chosen* upgrade will surface uncapped per-occurrence conditions. See `docs/BACKLOG.md` §3.
  - **Bug caught mid-run:** the Weakness Lens set `expiresAtSlot: 0`, which is not "no expiry" but "expires at slot 0" — a party-wide +4 that stayed up for the rest of the battle off one card. Now uses the same `WEAK_POINT_SLOTS` lifetime as the skill path.
  - v0.3 and v0.4 are untouched: the camp is gated behind `hasCamp()`, gem/item fields are inert in every other ruleset, and the v0.3 hard baseline still measures 61.0%. No UI yet — the camp is engine- and sim-only, and `DecisionPanel` renders nothing for its decisions rather than crashing.
  - New: `src/content/items.ts`, `src/engine/clock/items.ts`, `src/engine/clock/camp.ts`, `tests/camp.test.ts` (22 tests). `npm test` **268/268 passing**, `npm run typecheck` clean, `npm run balance -- <n> <tier> v0.5` now runs.

- **v0.4.5 — boss stays centered, party clusters instead of spreading on tall screens** — follow-up to v0.4.4 from a second look at the same screenshot: the boss was still bottom-anchored rather than centered, and a portrait-shaped window stretched the four heroes down the whole column instead of keeping them together.
  - **The boss is now centered, not bottom-anchored.** v0.4.4 fixed the HP plate floating away from the boss by bottom-aligning the sprite inside its `flex-1` wrap (`items-end`) — but flex-1 also means that wrap claims the *entire* leftover column height, so the "empty space" from a sprite smaller than its max always collected above the boss, never below it. Capped the wrap at the sprite's own min/max-height in real pixels instead of letting it fill the column, so `justify-center` on the outer button now has actual slack to split evenly above and below — the boss (+ its attached plate) floats in the vertical middle of its column at every window size.
  - **Heroes cluster instead of spreading down a tall column**, capped at half the available height (`max-h-[50%]`) so a portrait viewport turns the leftover space into margin above/below via `justify-center` rather than stretching the party's 4 equal `flex-1` slots across the whole thing. Verified live at 400×2000: heroes sit in the center 50% with exactly 25.0%/25.0% margin top and bottom.
  - **First attempt at the cap used `max-height: max-content`, and it silently undersized both.** A percentage height evaluated inside a max-content computation resolves as `auto` per the CSS sizing spec, which collapsed `.boss-sprite`/`.hero-sprite` to their *min*-height instead of their max even with abundant room — the boss rendered at 175px instead of 245 on an ordinary 1280×720 window. Switched every cap to a real measured pixel value instead of the `max-content` keyword, which sidesteps that intrinsic-sizing pass entirely.
  - **Second bug, found by measuring rather than eyeballing**: once the group could shrink toward the hero floor, `min-h-0` on the wrap/slot elements let them shrink *past* `.hero-sprite`'s own min-height — a flex item's `min-height:auto` doesn't propagate through nested flex containers, so the sprite (held to its 80px floor) overflowed a wrap that had been squeezed to 24px, overlapping the next hero down. Fixed by giving every nesting level (wrap, slot, group) its own explicit min-height instead of `min-h-0`, each numerically justified from `.hero-sprite`'s real 80/112 bounds and the measured 20px plate height (`HERO_SLOT_MIN`/`HERO_SLOT_MAX`/`HERO_GROUP_MIN` in `HeroFigures.tsx`) — confirmed no overlap across four viewports via a script checking every sprite's rect against its neighbor's.
  - **`battle-stage`'s own floor was too low to fit its own contents.** 4 heroes at their floor height plus plates need ~406px; the stage's min-height only guaranteed 380px on desktop, so the party clipped against the stage's `overflow-hidden` on an ordinary 1280×720 window — not just the portrait edge case this session started from. `HERO_GROUP_MIN` is now exported and the stage sizes off of it directly (`max(HERO_GROUP_MIN + 40px, 46vh)`) instead of a hand-picked constant, so the two can't drift apart again.
  - Verified with a repeatable rect-measuring script (not just screenshots) across 1280×720, 1280×400, 400×1400, 400×2000, 375×812, and 320×568: zero clipping, zero overlap, and the margin scales from ~2.6% on cramped windows up to exactly 25%/25% once the screen is tall enough to afford it.
  - 246 tests passing, typecheck clean. Layout and sprite-sheet CSS only — no engine or balance change.


- **v0.4.4 — battle stage sizing and sprite polish** — reported from a screenshot: the boss HP bar floated away from the boss, characters looked tiny at some window sizes, boss sprites seemed to shrink mid-action, and idle animation looked like it was "walking backward." Four separate causes, one screenshot.
  - **HP plate now hugs the boss instead of the stage floor.** `BossFigure`'s sprite wrapper centered the sprite vertically (`items-center`) inside a `flex-1` area, so on any viewport taller than the sprite's own max-height it floated mid-air with a growing gap above the HP plate, which stayed pinned to the bottom of the column. Changed to `items-end` so the sprite's bottom edge — and therefore the HP plate directly below it — track together at every viewport height.
  - **Sprites had a max-height but no floor.** `.hero-sprite` (112px cap) and `.boss-sprite` (245px cap) shrank with zero limit whenever `battle-stage` ran out of room, and `battle-stage`'s own desktop floor was only 190px — comfortably smaller than a 4-row hero column needs even at reduced size. Added `min-height` floors (80px hero, 175px boss) and raised `battle-stage`'s min-height (190px → 380px on desktop, 260px → 340px on tablet), with the page's desktop scroll lock changed from `overflow-hidden` to `overflow-y-auto` so a genuinely short window scrolls instead of forcing sprites below their floor.
  - **Measured, not guessed, the "shrinking/walking" idle.** Cropped every idle frame's alpha bounding box for all three bosses: the body's horizontal center swings 33–40px across the 4-frame loop for Ragorath, Somnivar, *and* Aurelius alike (e.g. Ragorath: 168 → 164 → 182 → 142px), while the vertical bbox stays put — a per-frame authoring drift, not a CSS bug, and exactly what reads as a wobble/backward-step on an infinite loop. Recentered all three sheets' idle row programmatically (shift each frame to the shared average center, clamped so nothing crops past the cell edge) and re-saved lossless — no repaint, no dimension change.
  - **Softened the action/hit punch dip.** `boss-action-punch`, `boss-hit-recoil`, and their hero equivalents bottomed out at `scale(.96)` before overshooting past 1 — legible as "flinch" on a still image, but stacked on top of the idle drift above it read as the sprite shrinking every time it acted. Dropped the sub-1 scale from all four keyframes; the punch/recoil translateX motion is unchanged.
  - **Charging sprite for the wait, not just the payoff.** `fighter.pending.skillId` already tracks a declared-but-not-landed skill (e.g. Liora's Fireball sits pending for several slots before it resolves), but `HeroSprite` only ever read `actionFlash?.skillId` — the brief resolve-time flash — so the wait itself always rendered as generic idle row 0. `HeroFigures` now falls back to `f.pending?.skillId` when there's no live flash, and `HeroSprite` plays that row on the looping idle animation (not the one-shot action punch) whenever there's a row with no `actionId` behind it — so a charging Fireball holds its casting pose for the whole wait, using art that already existed.
  - Verified live: HP plate sits flush against the boss at 1280×720 down to 1280×400 (which now scrolls rather than compressing further); mobile portrait unaffected; declared Kit's Sharp Shooting and Luna's Aura Smite mid-battle and confirmed both held their skill's row (`--sprite-row: 50%`/`75%`) on the idle-loop class while pending, then correctly dropped back to row 0 the instant they resolved. 246 tests passing, typecheck clean. No engine or balance change — sizing, alignment, and sprite-sheet asset touch-ups only.


- **v0.4.3 — on-screen effects and the battle log now advance on one clock** — reported as "the effect and the log don't line up; sometimes the log is ahead, sometimes behind". It was structural rather than flaky, and the "sometimes/sometimes" was the tell.
  - The log line and its effect were always *pushed* together, in the same notify — that part was never wrong. What differed was how long each stayed up: **effects had two fixed lifetimes (`FLASH_MS` 1400, `POPUP_MS` 1100) while the gap to the next log line was `eventDelay(ev, animSpeedMs)`**, which varies both by event type and by the player's speed setting. The two therefore agreed at **no setting at all**:
    - at 500 ms/event a flash outlived its own line by roughly **2.8 further lines** — the picture visibly trailed the text;
    - at 2000 ms/event it died **600 ms before** the next line arrived — the board sat blank while the log still showed the move;
    - marker ticks (0.22×) skewed it differently again mid-battle, which is why it read as drifting rather than as a constant offset.
  - **Fix: an effect's lifetime is now derived from the same delay the drain loop sleeps before revealing the next event** (`effectDurationFor`), plus a small fixed 200 ms tail so the handoff between consecutive effects never leaves a blank frame. They are one clock by construction now, at every speed and for every event type, rather than two constants that happened to be close at one setting.
  - **Verified live, not just in principle**: instrumented a running battle at both speed extremes and measured how many log lines each flash spans. Now ≤1 at both 500 ms and 2000 ms (avg 0.25–0.30). `tests/effectSync.test.ts` pins both failure directions — an effect may never die before its line stops being the newest, and may never outlive it by more than the tail — across every entry in `ANIM_DELAY_OPTIONS`.
  - 245 tests passing, typecheck and build clean. No engine or balance change: this is playback pacing only.


- **v0.4.2 — first balance run on the v0.4.0 ruleset, and the bug it found** — `npm run balance` now takes a ruleset flag (`v0.3` | `v0.4`) and reports per-ailment application, tick and damage counts. Flags are matched by value rather than position: they were positional at first, and `balance 5000 v0.4` silently ran v0.3 with a bot tier named "v0.4" while printing a v0.3 header. A mislabelled balance number is worse than none, so unrecognised flags now throw.
  - **What it measures.** Bots can only draft the base four, so a v0.4 run is *not* a measurement of Chrono/Kage/Morvane. It measures one thing cleanly — what boss ailments do to a party otherwise identical to the v0.3 baseline, with the roster held constant across both runs.
  - **Ailments cost the party ~10pp**: hard 62.3% → 51.7%, medium 76.1% → 69.0% (5,000 games each). Daze is the loudest at 10.3 applications per game and is pure tempo tax with no damage attached, so it is invisible in every damage-based readout.
  - **🔴 The run found a real bug: doom had no counterplay whatsoever.** Its rules text has always read "unless it is cleansed first", but `cleanseAilments` existed in the engine **with no caller anywhere** — nothing in the game could cleanse. Doom was a delayed execution rather than a status: it lands on the party's score leader and kills them 8 slots later. Measured **5,413 applied, 2,221 reaching zero — a 41% kill rate**, and 25,052 of the system's 40,419 total ailment damage came from doom alone. `tests/ailmentBalance.test.ts` now asserts the design invariant (any outright-lethal ailment must be flagged `cleansable`) so the hole cannot reopen with new boss content.
  - **The wrong turn on the way to the fix, recorded because it is the more useful half.** Cleansing was put on Heal first — thematically obvious, measurably wrong. Once bots were taught to value it, hard win rate went **54.1% → 47.8%**: the party stopped dying to doom and started losing to the clock instead. **This is §8.0's Guard-v1 lesson reproducing exactly, on a different card, five versions later** — a support action that produces no damage cannot pay its own ⏱ in this ruleset.
  - **Shipped instead: Aura Smite cleanses the whole party.** It is Luna's light-element attack, and the element table already had light as "pierces armor + washes off status", so the answer to a status now arrives *attached to* damage rather than instead of it. Doom deaths 2,221 → 1,030 without surrendering the damage race, and Luna never has to choose between healing and saving.
  - **51.7% is a bot number, not a ceiling.** The spread across three heuristic variants (47.8% / 51.7% / 54.1%) mostly measures the bot's understanding — the very thing that cannot be trusted here — so heuristic tuning was stopped deliberately rather than pushed further. The structural fix is the deliverable; the percentage is not.
  - v0.3 verified untouched at 62.3% with identical win shares. 240 tests passing, typecheck and build clean. Full write-up in `docs/BALANCE_NOTES.md`.
